Evaluation · the portal and the hosted analysis

Bring your own codec, and measure the mechanism in it.

The portal is the confidential evaluation environment: organisation accounts, a versioned agreement, gated artifact delivery and an append-only audit log. The hosted analysis is the other half: post a sequence and get the same figures the benchmark writes, without building a toolchain first.

Nothing here is a request for a demo and nothing is priced. The portal is the way in if your organisation needs the package under an agreement; the analysis is the way in if you want to see the numbers first.

The two doors

The portal, and the analysis

The portal

For an organisation that needs the reference implementation, the trained predictor, the benchmark harness and the patent materials under a confidential evaluation agreement. An organisation registers, a user signs the agreement, a request names the codec to test against, and the gated artifacts become downloadable.

Every step writes to an append-only audit log, and a download link is signed, time-limited and bound to the artifact's digest, so it cannot be edited to fetch something else or replayed after it expires.

The hosted analysis

For seeing what the measurement says before committing to anything. Post a Y4M sequence and the service encodes it in both signalling modes, sweeps the margin, and returns the figures: the rate, the distortion, the prediction mismatch and the predictability ceiling.

It is the same code the repository ships, running on one free instance, and it stores nothing: the sequence lives in the request and is dropped with it.

Why an evaluation rather than a paper

The published numbers are a starting point, not a conclusion

The published numbers come from GIRC, a research codec with a small mode set and integer-sample motion, and they put the mechanism at a 0.236% saving on the luma plane and 0.149% on the whole frame, on ten sequences of twelve. That result is a starting point for an argument, not the end of one.

A codec team with a real VVC or AV1 encoder can test the mechanism where the transform set is larger, where the residual coding is richer, and where inter prediction is in play. Those are exactly the three things most likely to change the answer, and none of them can be tested with GIRC's mode set.

What you receive

The whole thing, published

Reference codec

The full Rust implementation with both signalling modes behind one interface, its test suite, and the oracle that checks the transform arithmetic against VVenC bit for bit.

Trained predictor

The model, the training set, its checksum and the pipeline that produced it, including the predictability ceiling over the same features.

Benchmark harness

The tool behind the published figures, with the anchor codecs and the Bjontegaard implementation, so the evaluation is reproducible on your own material.

Patent materials

The disclosure, the claim set, the drawings and the prior-art analysis, with the claim text of the ten references it was read against.

It is published, not gated

The patent materials are in the repository rather than behind an agreement, because a claim is only worth as much as somebody else's ability to check it. The agreement exists for the evaluation of your own results, not for access to the claims.

What we ask of you

Report honestly, including when it does not help

Run it against your encoder

The benchmark takes your content and your encoder, and the comparison runs on both rather than only on ours.

Report the negative results

Including when the mechanism does not help. A negative result is worth more to the filing than a flattering one, because it establishes the mechanism's real envelope.

Say what you changed

The features, the margin and the grain of the signalling are all parameters. A result that moved one of them is a different result and has to be reported as one.

Talk to us

Especially about the case where the mechanism fails. That is where the claim set has to be drawn carefully, and your evidence is what draws it.
