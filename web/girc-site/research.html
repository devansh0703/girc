The method

The mechanism, and the one thing it does that the standard does not

VVC already has implicit multiple transform selection: it derives the transform type from already-coded information, the block size and the neighbour availability, and spends zero bits on it. GIRC generalises that. Instead of deriving the transform from a fixed table indexed by syntax, it predicts it from the geometry the decoder has reconstructed, and when the prediction is wrong it codes a correction relative to the prediction rather than the type itself.

That second part is the mechanism, and it is what makes the design worth having. The naive form spends a bin on the correction and then the full type again, which is three bins against the explicit mode's two. Coding the correction relative to the prediction brings it back to two, so an override never costs more than the signalling it replaces.

The result

What was measured, and what it does not show

The published result is −0.236% on the luma plane at the margin of 50, over twelve CIF sequences at four quantization parameters. It is a small win, and the reason is measurable: the transform mode is a near-tie in rate-distortion terms, so the predictability ceiling of the feature set is 50.65% against a 50.10% majority class. There is little for a predictor to predict.

The mechanism is therefore a signalling-removal technique whose value grows with the number of transform choices and with how evenly they are distributed. A codec with a richer residual coder, where the modes separate more, is where it should pay. That is an open question, and it is stated as one.

The prior art

What the ten references disclose, and what none of them reach

The claim text of ten references was extracted from the publications themselves and read. Every quotation in the claim chart is checked against the extracted text by the test suite, so the chart cannot quote a document it did not read.

Element Found in
E1 · implicit derivation of the transform 10 of 10 references
E2 · reconstructed-neighbourhood geometry 3 of 10 references
E3 · per-axis selection 6 of 10 references
E4 · the rate-distortion margin 0 of 10 references
E5 · trained prediction 2 of 10 references
E6 · prediction-relative correction 2 of 10 references

No claim in any of them reads a threshold, a margin, a sub-optimal choice, or a trade of distortion against signalling. The closest prior art reports the same kind of result: InterDigital's EP3977732B1 reports +0.34% overall for its implicit transform design, against an MTS-disabled anchor.

What this is not

A claim-level prior-art search is not a freedom-to-operate opinion. The national phase filings of the families in the set were not searched, and a counsel-led search is still required before anything is filed or licensed.
