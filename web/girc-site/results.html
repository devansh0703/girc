MEASURED ON XIPH.ORG CIF · 12 SEQUENCES · 10 FRAMES · QP 22–37

The mechanism saves 0.239% of the bitrate.

Twelve CIF sequences, ten frames each, four QP points, and the inferred mode comes out 0.239% smaller than the explicit mode on the luma plane, and 0.156% smaller on the whole frame. It is smaller on ten sequences of the twelve. The margin sweep below is the whole trade: the rate the encoder gives up in distortion, against the rate it saves in signalling.

Method

Every number below comes from the same repository, with the same sequences and the same quantization parameter sweep. GIRC runs in both signalling modes over a four-point QP sweep, and the Bjontegaard delta rate is computed by cubic polynomial interpolation over the overlapping PSNR range, as the JVET common test conditions define it. The two GIRC modes differ in exactly one thing: how the transform mode reaches the decoder.

The transform mode is VVC's, not an invention of this repository's: the plain DCT-II, or one of the four per-axis combinations of DCT-VIII and DST-VII that ITU-T H.266 defines as multiple transform selection. The explicit mode codes the mode the way the standard's mts_idx syntax does, which is what the decoder of a real codec would have to read.

The anchors are the real encoders: x265 for HEVC, SVT-AV1 for AV1 and VVenC 1.14.0 for VVC, each coding the same ten frames of the same file, all three planes, and measured by the same PSNR code.

Does the learned predictor earn its keep?

A claim about a trained predictor has to beat the alternative that needs no training and no model: always predict the plain DCT-II. The benchmark runs both over the same content, the same quantization parameters and the same margin, and reports them in the same file.

Predictor Mean BD-rate, luma Better on
Constant, always the plain DCT-II −0.120% 10 of 12
The shipped learned model −0.239% 10 of 12

The learned model is ahead on nine of the twelve sequences, and the gap between the two is 0.119 percentage points of BD-rate, which is twice the mechanism's own effect. The model is one multinomial logistic regression over the joint mode, fitted on 760,320 real block records from the twelve sequences.

How predictable is the transform?

The predictor's accuracy is the mechanism's ceiling, so the training pipeline measures it two ways: the shipped logistic regression, and a gradient-boosted tree fitted over the same features as an upper bound on what any model over them can achieve.

Measure Accuracy Reading
Majority class (always DCT-II) 53.50% The baseline any predictor must beat, over the five modes
Gradient-boosted ceiling, joint mode 53.89% What these features support for the mode as a whole
Shipped model, over the joint mode 53.41% 92.3% on the plain DCT-II, 3.6% to 17.9% on the four others

760,320 blocks, collected from the encoder's own rate-distortion search over twelve sequences at four QP points, so the labels are the encoder's real choices and the features are what the decoder will actually see.

The honest reading. The joint mode is barely predictable: the ceiling of 53.9% is barely above the 53.5% majority class. The transform choice is driven by the block's own residual, which the decoder has not seen, and the geometry of the neighbourhood constrains it only weakly. The rate-distortion choice between the modes is close to a tie, so there is little for a predictor to predict.

An earlier version modelled the two axes separately and composed them, because each axis on its own was well above the majority class. The composed mode came out below it. The five modes are not a product space, so composing two independent per-axis models cannot recover the joint distribution, and the measurement showed it losing. The shipped predictor is one model over the five modes.

Even so, the saving is 0.239%, not a headline number. The explicit signalling is already cheap for a context-adaptive coder to code, and the mechanism removes an overhead that is close to the distortion it costs to remove it. The margin sweep is the honest account of where that balance sits.

What would change the answer

Inter prediction

The residual of a predicted block carries the motion vector's structure, which is far more directional than an intra residual and far more predictable from the causal neighbourhood.

A wider causal window

The features read a 16-sample window. A larger one, or a learned representation of the neighbourhood, would raise the predictability ceiling.

Signalling at a coarser grain

Signalling the transform once per region rather than once per block would make the prediction problem much easier and the overhead much larger.
