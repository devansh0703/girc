METHOD · TRANSFORM-TYPE INFERENCE

Deriving the transform from what the decoder already has.

The mechanism is a substitution. Where a conventional codec spends bits to say which transform it used, GIRC has the decoder work it out, and the encoder only has to correct it when the guess is wrong.

The bitstream

GIRC's bitstream is its own research format, not VVC-conformant. That is deliberate: the mechanism has to be measured with everything else held constant, and a conformant bitstream would force the comparison to inherit every other design decision of the standard. 

"GIRC"        4 bytes magic
version       u16, little endian
flags         u16, little endian  bit 0: inferred signalling
width         u32, little endian
height        u32, little endian
qp            u8
payload len   u32, little endian
payload       range-coded words

The flags field records the signalling mode, so a stream is self-describing: the two modes have different syntax, and a decoder that guessed wrong would desynchronise.

The syntax

The explicit mode codes the transform type with a truncated binary code: the first type in the canonical order costs one bin and the other two cost two.

The inferred mode codes one bin saying whether the decoder's prediction was right. When it was wrong, one further bin selects between the two transforms the prediction ruled out. The ordering of the remaining two is canonical, so the selection is unambiguous.

That second bin is what makes the design worth having. The naive form of the mechanism spends a bin on the correction and then the full type again, which is three bins against the explicit mode's two. Coding the correction relative to the prediction brings it back to two, so an override never costs more than the signalling it replaces.

The features

The decoder reads a 16-sample square window ending at the block's top-left corner. Every sample in it has already been reconstructed, so reading it costs nothing in signalling. The features are:

Feature Count What it measures
orientation histogram 8 The gradient orientation field, over the full circle
gradient energy 1 The total magnitude, log-scaled
dominance 1 The strongest bin's share of the total
isotropy 1 The entropy of the histogram
reference coverage 1 How much of the immediate reference is present
horizontal, vertical energy 2 The per-axis gradient magnitude, log-scaled
axis correlation 1 Whether the two axes agree: an edge or a corner
quantization parameter 1 Which transform wins depends on it
intra mode 10 One-hot; decoded before the transform mode
neighbour modes 20 One-hot per neighbour, over the five modes: left, above, above-left, above-right

The gradient is taken with central differences over a genuine two-dimensional window. Restricting it to the two reference lines — the top row and the left column — throws away the diagonal information that distinguishes the transforms, and leaves four of the eight orientation bins permanently empty.

The neighbour context is free: the decoder already knows every already-coded neighbour's transform mode, because it decoded them. It is also the context the codec literature uses, since the transform choice is strongly spatially correlated.

The intra mode is the strongest single signal, and it is available on both sides: the mode is decoded before the transform type, so the predictor may use it. The quantization parameter is available from the bitstream header.

The predictor

The predictor models the joint mode: one multinomial logistic regression over the five modes. An earlier version modelled the two axes separately and composed them, because each axis on its own was well above the majority class — about 53.9% against 50.1% — and the composed mode came out below it, at 48.8%. The five modes are not a product space: the plain DCT-II appears on an axis only in the mode where both axes are the plain DCT-II, and the four multiple transform selection modes are correlated. Composing two independent per-axis models cannot recover the joint distribution, and the measurement showed it losing. One model over the five modes is both simpler and better.

The regression is a weight vector and a bias per mode, and the prediction is the highest logit. It is a pure function of its weights and the feature vector, so the encoder and the decoder always agree.

The features include the block's own prediction plane. The encoder chooses the transform that best compacts the residual, and the residual inherits the prediction's directional structure, so the prediction's orientation is the most direct evidence of the transform the encoder will want. Both sides have the prediction before they need the transform type, because the intra mode and the motion vector are both decoded first. For an inter block the prediction comes from the previous frame at a motion-compensated position, so it carries information the causal window of the current frame does not have at all.

The model is trained on the encoder's own decisions. The collection pass runs the encoder and records, for every block, the geometry features and the transform the rate-distortion search actually chose. The training set is therefore the encoder's behaviour and not a proxy for it, and the features are taken from the reconstructed neighbourhood, exactly as the decoder will see them.

The pipeline also reports the predictability ceiling: a gradient-boosted tree fitted over the same features gives an upper bound on what any model over them can achieve. A report can then state what the features can support rather than only what the shipped model achieves.

The margin

The mechanism's free parameter is the margin: the amount of rate-distortion cost, the encoder is willing to give up to avoid signalling the transform type. When the predicted transform is within the margin of the search's own choice, the encoder takes the predicted one and spends nothing.

The margin is the honest form of the mechanism. Without it the encoder takes the strictly optimal transform and overrides whenever the prediction differs, and the inferred mode is reliably larger than the explicit one, because the signalling it removes is smaller than the distortion it costs. The margin is what lets the two balance.

The margin applies only to the inferred mode. The explicit mode stays a clean rate-distortion optimum, so the comparison between them is not confounded by the explicit baseline being deliberately handicapped.

What is held constant

The two modes share the intra prediction, the quantization, the transform arithmetic, the residual coding and the entropy coder. They differ in one place: how the transform type reaches the decoder. A difference in the measured rate is therefore attributable to the signalling mechanism alone.

The transform arithmetic is the real thing. The integer transforms are bit-exact against Fraunhofer HHI's VVenC 1.14.0, checked by a test that drives the reference implementation's own kernels and compares the results bit for bit across all twelve transform and size combinations.
