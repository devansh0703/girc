## Problem Statement

A video codec can gain bitrate by *inferring* transform decisions instead of
signalling them. VVC already proved the shape of this in one narrow case:
implicit Multiple Transform Selection derives the transform type from
already-coded information (block size, neighbour availability) and spends zero
bits on transform-type signalling, and implicit MIP derives the matrix mode
the same way. The general form — infer the *geometry* of a transform from
already-decoded spatial structure, then signal only a correction — is not in
the standard.

The commercial shape from the brief is an IP business, not a product company:
sell a protected research package (patent filing, reference code, training
method, benchmark results) to codec teams under confidential evaluation
agreement, then license or sell to an established codec or IP firm. The first
customer pays for an evaluation against their own codec work.

The wedge the brief identifies is ~20 reachable global codec implementers at
~$750K/yr average evaluation and patent-licence value (~$15M), inside a
web-verified global video codec market of $2.3B.

**The gap that matters:** the brief scores "Market 3/10" and "AI-runnable
3/10", and says plainly that "our market research on this exact method was
moderately thin". A codec team will not buy a claim. They will buy a
reproducible result they can run themselves. The deliverable therefore has to
be *executable evidence*, not a document.

## Status Quo

Codec teams today compare a proposed method against accepted test methods
(CTC conditions, VTM/VVenC, libaom, x265). Claimed gains routinely disappear
when real decoding limits apply. A codec team's internal process today:

1. Reproduce the claim on their own content and their own hardware.
2. Check decoder complexity is acceptable.
3. Only then consider licensing or a standards proposal.

Step 1 is expensive: a proposal without runnable code costs the evaluating
team weeks. That gap — claim vs. reproducible artifact — is the wedge.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

**The person:** a codec research lead or head of video architecture at a
semiconductor or video-processing company (segment 1) or a streaming platform
/ encoding-infrastructure vendor (segment 2). Their budget line is research,
licensing, or standards funds. They are evaluated on standards-essential
patent contribution, so a *credible, reproducible* prior-art-defensible
method is worth more to them than a large bitrate number alone.

**The smallest thing worth paying for this week:** a downloadable, runnable
evaluation kit that (a) demonstrates the geometry-inference mechanism on a
real codec build, (b) re-runs the benchmark on the buyer's own content,
(c) reports BD-rate and decode complexity, and (d) carries the training
method and data-rights records needed to file a patent without redoing the
work. Plus a confidential evaluation agreement that lets them run it without
publishing.

## Constraints

- **Solo founder, ~$300K capital, 9 months to time-to-market.** No lab, no
  fabrication, no large operating team. This is an IP business.
- **The claim must survive independent codec-lab verification** before any
  public release. "Lab gains may vanish under real decoding limits" is the
  brief's own stated risk.
- **Decoder complexity must be acceptable.** An inference mechanism that
  doubles decode cost is worthless in a shipping codec.
- **Data rights must be clean.** The package includes training data and
  benchmark content, so every input needs a recorded licence.
- **Codec teams already license video patents.** They will not buy a research
  result that is not protectable. The package must include the patent
  package, not just the code.
- **Standards timing.** New codec designs are still open, so a protected and
  tested method can still enter standards work while choices remain unsettled.

## Premises

1. **The mechanism must be real and measurable, not a paper claim.** If the
   geometry-inference tool cannot show a reproducible BD-rate delta against
   VVenC/VTM on standard test conditions, there is no product. VERIFIED: this
   is the premise the whole project rests on, and it is the thing the build
   must prove first.
2. **An executable artifact out-sells a document.** Codec evaluators
   reproduce, they do not read. The kit is the product; the patent package is
   what makes it licensable. AGREED.
3. **The buyer is a small, finite, reachable set (~20 codec implementers),
   so distribution is outbound research, not growth marketing.** AGREED.
4. **The honest end-state is a licensed or sold patent, not a standalone
   company.** The brief says this outright; the "upside remains narrow
   without more codec inventions". AGREED — which means the toolkit has to be
   reusable for the *next* method, or the business dies after one patent.
5. **Two products, not one.** The brief describes a single patent package.
   The user requires two separate products, each with its own website. The
   defensible split: the *research toolkit* (open, drives adoption and proves
   the mechanism) and the *evaluation + licensing service* (the commercial
   vehicle that sells confidential evaluation and rights). The brief's own
   "vehicle: LICENSE OR SELL" and "wins through evaluations and licenses, not
   a standalone product" support exactly this split.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Single monorepo, two products, one shared core
One Rust codec crate implementing the geometry-inference mechanism, a
benchmark harness, a training pipeline, an evaluation portal, and two
marketing sites. The research core is shared; the two products are separate
deliverables with separate websites.

- Summary: build GIRC (the codec + toolkit) as the technical core, then
  build GIRC Evaluate (the portal) on top of it, plus two Astro product
  sites.
- Effort: L
- Risk: Medium
- Pros:
  - The mechanism gets proven first, in code, before any packaging.
  - The portal can serve real benchmark results produced by the harness
    rather than hand-written numbers.
  - One repo, one CI, one licence surface — matches the user's requirement.
  - The toolkit generalises to the next method, so premise 4 is served.
- Cons:
  - Scope is large for one founder; sequencing discipline required.
  - Two products share a codebase, so a bug in core hits both.
- Reuses: VVenC (Fraunhofer, Clear BSD) as the reference VVC codec and
  transform-matrix source; constriction (MIT/Apache-2.0) for entropy coding;
  FAU-LMS Bjontegaard reference implementation for BD-rate; FFmpeg (LGPL) for
  YUV handling and anchor encodes.

### Approach B: Sell the patent package only, no code product
Research the mechanism, file, and license. No toolkit.

- Summary: pure IP play — analysis, patent filing, licensing conversations.
- Effort: S
- Risk: High
- Pros: lowest cost, matches "license or sell" literally.
- Cons: rejects premise 2. Codec evaluators need a runnable artifact; a
  patent with no reproducible result is worth a fraction of one with. Also
  fails the user's explicit two-product + two-website requirement.
- Reuses: nothing new.

### Approach C: Standalone codec product (ship a better codec)
Build a competing codec and sell it.

- Summary: new codec, sell licences to encode/decode.
- Effort: XL
- Risk: Very high
- Pros: large theoretical market ($2.3B).
- Cons: directly contradicts the brief ("this is not a standalone company",
  "the realistic route, not a standalone product") and the user's brief.
  9 months and $300K cannot take on VVC/AV1. Rejected.
- Reuses: nothing.

## Recommended Approach

**Approach A**, because it satisfies premise 1 first: the build starts with a
bit-exact codec that implements geometry-inference transform prediction, and
the benchmark harness that measures it against VVenC on real test sequences.
If the mechanism does not produce a reproducible gain, the project fails
early and cheaply, which is the correct failure mode. Everything else
(portal, sites, patent package) sits downstream of that proof.

### Product split

**Product 1 — GIRC (Geometry-Inference Reference Codec).**
An open research toolkit: a Rust codec implementing the mechanism, a
benchmark harness that re-runs against real anchors on real content, a
training pipeline that produces the inference model, and reproducible
benchmark data. Website: `girc` site. Its job is to make the mechanism
reproducible by anyone, which is what makes the patent defensible and
adoptable.

**Product 2 — GIRC Evaluate (confidential evaluation portal).**
The commercial vehicle: an organisation-facing web app where a codec team
requests an evaluation, signs a versioned NDA, downloads gated artifacts
(reference implementation, model weights, benchmark harness, patent package
drafts), runs the evaluation against its own codec, submits results, and
tracks the licensing conversation. Admin side reviews requests and issues
evaluation reports. Website: `evaluate` site. This is where the brief's
"first customer pays to test the method against its own codec work" and
"confidential evaluation agreements" actually live.

### Mechanism (the thing being built and measured)

VVC's implicit MTS shows the general trick: transform type is a function of
already-coded information, so it needs no signalling bits. GIRC generalises
it to *geometry*:

- The encoder, at a transform unit, has a candidate set of transform
  **modes**, each of which is a per-axis pair drawn from VVC's actual MTS
  set (DCT-II, DST-VII, DCT-VIII), taken from the spec matrices. The shipped
  codec uses five modes: (DCT-II,DCT-II), (DST-VII,DST-VII),
  (DCT-VIII,DST-VII), (DST-VII,DCT-VIII), (DCT-VIII,DCT-VIII).
- Instead of signalling which one won, the decoder **predicts** the mode
  from spatial structure that both sides already have: the reconstructed
  neighbour samples around the unit, whose local gradient/edge orientation
  indicates which transform family will compress the residual best.
- The encoder then signals only a **correction**, and only when its RDO
  choice is not the predicted one. The correction is relative to the
  prediction, and selects among the four non-predicted modes. Zero bits
  when the prediction is right.
- The predictor is a trained model — one multinomial logistic regression over
  the joint mode, reading a 2-D structure tensor of the causal neighbourhood,
  the decoded neighbours' own transform modes, the motion context, and the
  block's own prediction plane — shipped as a frozen, versioned artifact, so
  encoder and decoder are guaranteed bit-identical.

  The model is over the joint mode rather than over the two axes, and that
  was measured. Each axis on its own predicted well above the majority class
  (about 54.6% against 49.8%), and the two were then composed into a mode —
  and the composed mode came out *below* the majority, at 48.3%. The five
  modes are not a product space: the plain DCT-II appears on an axis only in
  the mode where both axes are the plain DCT-II, and the four multiple
  transform selection modes are correlated. Composing two independent
  per-axis models cannot recover the joint distribution, and the measurement
  showed it losing. One model over the five modes is simpler and better.

  The prediction plane is in the feature set because the encoder chooses the
  transform that best compacts the residual, and the residual inherits the
  prediction's directional structure. Both sides have the prediction before
  they need the transform type, since the intra mode and the motion vector
  are both decoded first. For an inter block the prediction comes from the
  previous frame at a motion-compensated position, so it carries
  information the causal window of the current frame does not have at all.

This is precisely the brief's "geometry-transform inference lets a video codec
infer some transforms instead of sending each one", and it is precisely the
lineage's "matrix-based intra prediction" / "geometry transforms" prior art
space. It is also why the package needs a *training method* deliverable: the
model is part of the protected invention, and the training pipeline must be
reproducible for the patent to be defensible.

### The signalling is the standard's signalling

GIRC's explicit mode codes the transform type with the syntax ITU-T
H.266 uses for `mts_idx`. That is checked against the reference
implementation rather than asserted: the reference encoder is run with
multiple transform selection enabled, its syntax trace is read to get
the transform mode it actually chose for every transform unit it coded
— 187,834 of them on one CIF frame, covering four of the five modes —
and the bin sequence it writes for each is compared with GIRC's. They
agree on every mode, and the check is in `tools/check_mts_syntax.py`
and runs in CI.

The reason it matters is that the mechanism's argument is that the
transform-type signalling is redundant. That argument only means
something if the signalling being removed is the signalling a
conformant decoder has to read. With its own syntax, the measured
saving would be of a saving no conformant codec pays.

### The chroma planes, and why the mechanism does not touch them

ITU-T H.266 codes 4:2:0 content as three planes, and the chroma planes are
half the luma plane's width and height. GIRC codes all three, because the
benchmark compares GIRC's rate against the anchors' rate, and the anchors
code all three: comparing a luma-only GIRC rate against a 4:2:0 anchor rate
was not a comparison of the same thing.

What the standard fixes, and what the codec therefore does:

- **No chroma transform type is signalled.** H.266 clause 7.3.9 codes
  `transform_type` for the luma plane; the chroma transform type is
  inferred, and in 4:2:0 the inferred type is always the plain DCT-II.
  The reference encoder agrees: chroma transform selection is gated on
  `m_useChromaTS`, which defaults to off and is only enabled for 4:4:4
  content, and its intra search assigns `MTS_DCT2_DCT2` to the chroma
  planes directly. So the mechanism is a **luma** mechanism, which is
  exactly where the standard signals a transform type at all. This is not
  a limitation of the implementation; it is the shape of the invention.
- **The chroma quantization parameter is mapped, not equal.** It comes
  from the luma one through H.266's chroma quantization parameter mapping,
  derived in the same way the reference encoder derives it and checked
  against it for every quantization parameter.
- **The chroma grid is four by four chroma samples**, one chroma block
  per luma block, which is the relationship H.266's chroma transform
  blocks have to the luma ones, since a chroma sample covers four luma
  samples.
- **The chroma motion is derived from the luma motion**, as H.266 does,
  so the chroma planes cost no motion bits of their own.

The luma and chroma payloads are separate, and the container records each
payload's length. That is a measurement decision, not a compression one:
the mechanism changes the luma signalling and nothing else, so the luma
rate is the axis its effect is measured on, and the whole-frame rate is
the axis the anchors are compared on. The benchmark reports both.

### The honest measurement

The interesting number is not "our codec beats VVC" — it cannot, and claiming
it would be dishonest. The interesting number is the **signalling delta**:
bits saved on transform-type signalling per unit, and the **net BD-rate**
after paying for prediction errors with a fallback bit, measured against a
matched baseline that signals transform type explicitly, on identical content
and identical QPs, with decode complexity reported. GIRC implements both the
explicit-signalling baseline and the inference path so the comparison is
apples-to-apples, and the anchors (VVenC/VTM, x265, SVT-AV1) provide the
external reference point.

The patent claim that survives review is of the form: *a method whereby a
transform type is inferred at the decoder from the orientation statistics of
causally-decoded neighbouring samples, with a one-bit correction, achieving a
reduction in transform-signalling overhead at equivalent reconstruction
quality.* That claim is testable, and this project tests it.

## Open Questions

- Does the trained predictor generalise across content (natural video vs.
  screen content vs. animation)? The answer decides whether the model is one
  artifact or per-segment. **Still open.** The current model is trained on
  natural CIF content only.
- What is the true decode-complexity cost of the orientation feature
  extraction? If it is not near-free, the claim narrows to specific
  conditions. **Still open.** The feature extraction is a fixed 16x16
  structure tensor plus four neighbour mode lookups per block, and it is
  paid on the decode side for every block.
- Can the mechanism extend to inter prediction / matrix-weighted intra, or is
  it intra-only? **Answered: it extends to inter, and the extension is
  weaker than the intra case.** Integer-pel single-reference inter prediction
  is implemented and measured. The raw inter mismatch rate is consistently
  lower than the intra one, but the gain over the majority-class baseline is
  smaller, because the inter class distribution is more concentrated. So
  inter blocks are *easier* in the raw sense and *harder* to beat the
  baseline on. The IP is one patent covering a shared prediction path, not
  two.
- Who is the independent codec lab? The brief requires third-party
  verification before public release; a named lab is an early external
  dependency, not a late one. **Still open, and outside what code can do.**

## What the build actually found

These are the results that the design doc's own success criteria asked for,
as measured. The full numbers are in the root README and in
`research/patent-package/REPRODUCIBILITY.md`.

- The transform unit is bit-exact against Fraunhofer HHI's VVenC 1.14.0
  for all sixteen per-axis mode and size combinations, not just the twelve
  originally planned.
- The mechanism's net effect is a **small** win. The best margin in the
  full sweep, with the chroma planes coded and the comparison against
  whole-frame anchors, is −0.236% mean BD-rate on the luma plane and
  −0.149% on the whole frame, and it is negative on some sequences and
  positive on others. The three-transform result of −0.226% quoted in
  earlier notes was a different, superseded configuration and is not the
  headline.
- The reason the win is small is measurable. The joint five-class mode is
  barely predictable: the shipped model reaches 50.17% against a 50.10%
  majority class, and a gradient-boosted ceiling over the same features
  reaches 50.65%, because the rate-distortion choice between the modes is
  close to a tie. Predicting the two axes separately and composing them
  was tried and measured, and it was worse: each axis on its own was well
  above the majority class, about 53.9%, and the composed mode came out
  below it, at 48.8%. The five modes are not a product space, so
  composing two independent per-axis models cannot recover the joint
  distribution. The implementation predicts the joint mode with one
  model, and adds the block's own prediction plane to the features.

  The measurement is `tools/feature_study_v8.py`, which fits two per-axis
  models over the collected records and composes them with the product
  rule, against the joint model.
- The features have to be ones the decoder can compute, and enforcing
  that changed the measured result. An inter block carries a motion
  vector instead of an intra mode, so the decoder cannot know the mode
  the encoder would have chosen for it. A feature built from that mode
  lets the encoder predict from information the decoder does not have,
  and a model trained on it is flattered. Removing the feature dropped
  the measured accuracy from 53.4% to 50.2% and the whole-frame BD-rate
  from −0.156% to −0.149% in the other direction, and the benchmark now
  decodes every stream it encodes and refuses to report a result if the
  decoded picture differs from the encoder's reconstruction.
- The RD-cost margin is the part of the disclosure the prior art does not
  reach. The claim text of ten references was extracted and read, and the
  findings are:
  E1 implicit derivation in 10, E2 reconstructed-neighbourhood geometry in 3, E3 per-axis selection in 6, E4 the rate-distortion margin in 0, E5 trained prediction in 2, E6 prediction-relative correction in 2.
  Not one of them reads a threshold, a margin, a sub-optimal choice, or a
  trade of distortion against signalling. That is the surviving claim, and
  it is narrow.
- A claim-level search also found earlier art than the design doc assumed:
  Thomson Licensing's EP2991352A1 and US9357221B2 reach reconstructed-data-
  derived, trained, per-axis transform selection and prediction-relative
  correction. The broad claim space is materially narrower than the design
  doc assumed.

## Success Criteria

1. `girc` encodes and decodes bit-exactly (lossless round-trip on the
   reconstruction path, verified by test) and its output decodes to a frame
   matching the encoder's reconstruction.
2. A reproducible benchmark report on real, licensed test sequences
   (Xiph.Org derf collection) comparing: GIRC-explicit-signalling vs
   GIRC-inference (the mechanism's own delta), plus external anchors
   (VVenC, x265, SVT-AV1) for reference.
3. BD-rate computed with the FAU-LMS Bjontegaard reference method, with
   method and version recorded in the report.
4. Decode complexity reported (time per pixel) for both paths.
5. A trained, frozen, versioned inference model with a reproducible training
   pipeline (seed, dataset, hyperparameters recorded).
6. The evaluation portal completes a real end-to-end evaluation lifecycle:
   request → NDA → gated download → result submission → admin review →
   report issuance, with an audit trail.
7. A patent package with: technical disclosure, claim set, prior-art matrix
   (real, cited), data-rights records for every input, and a reproducibility
   manifest.
8. Two product websites, each with real content, building clean.

## Distribution Plan

- **GIRC (toolkit):** crates.io for the Rust crates + a GitHub Release
  binary for `girc` and `girc-bench` + the benchmark report as a static site.
  CI on GitHub Actions: cargo test, benchmark smoke run, portal test, both
  site builds.
- **GIRC Evaluate (portal):** containerised app (Dockerfile) deployed to a
  free-tier host; a self-hostable compose file for codec teams who need
  on-premise evaluation. Public site is static; the app is behind auth.
- **Patent package:** delivered only through the portal, to signed-in
  organisations under NDA, as a versioned, access-logged download.
